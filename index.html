Pattern Geometry

Per the AOML Early Stage G-IV Over-Storm pattern: a double circumnavigation nominally at 60/90 nmi, expandable to 90/120 nmi for safety. The figure-four leg length always matches the inner circumnavigation radius.
With the inner ring on, the default figure-four flies: start at point 20 (the ring's own last point) → cross the center → 21 (NW) → downwind of center → 22 (NE) → cross the center → out to 23 (SW). That's the standard 3-leg pattern, matching the real HRD mission convention. The 4th leg (SE, would be 24) is off by default since it just duplicates point 20's location — check it back on if you want that closing loop. With the inner ring off, there's no ring point to enter from, so it's flown as its own chain instead: enter NW, cross straight through the center to SE, transit directly over to NE, then cross through the center again to exit at SW. Each vertex lands exactly on the inner circumnavigation's diagonal point (leg length = inner radius) when the inner ring is shown. Midpoint (M) dropsondes, if enabled, sit at the true midpoint between the center fix and each vertex.
Uncheck a leg for safety (per AOML guidance, one crossing leg can be dropped). Uncheck midpoints to match mission plans that fly straight vertex-to-vertex, like the sample GABRIELLE plan. Figure-four points are numbered automatically, continuing right after the last circumnavigation point in use.

Export to Windy.com

Opens Windy's Distance & Planning tool with the flight sequence plotted as a track (departure/arrival airports and midpoint dropsondes excluded — just the ring and figure-four vertices). Any point the route revisits is kept in, since Windy breaks the track if a repeated point is dropped. No overlay or time is forced — Windy opens with its own default model/time bar so you can pick those yourself.
